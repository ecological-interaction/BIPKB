##### 5. Scientific distinctions and inference safeguards

| Distinction | Correct interpretation for candidate inventory | Evidence needed before strong claim |
|---|---|---|
| Taxon vs individual | Species identity is not an individual organism observation | Identification, taxonomic concept/version, organism or sample ID |
| Trait definition vs measured value | A trait concept, observation, species mean and model estimate differ | Measurement method, units, sampling context, aggregation and uncertainty |
| Phenotype vs functional trait | Phenotype is broad observable state; functional trait has an ecological-performance framing | Operational trait definition and empirical relevance |
| Plasticity vs adaptation | Environment-associated within-genotype phenotype change differs from inherited evolutionary response | Genetic design, common garden/reciprocal transplant or longitudinal evidence |
| Indicator vs biomarker vs sentinel | Indicator relationship, measurable biological response and selected warning organism are different roles | Target condition, mechanism, calibration, purpose |
| Occurrence vs absence | Presence record is not a designed absence survey | Sampling effort, detectability and survey design |
| Correlation vs causation | Environmental association alone does not establish a unique cause | Confounder controls, intervention/replication or convergent evidence |
| Individual vs assemblage indicator | Community indices may use composition without any individual phenotype | Index formula, taxa, reference conditions and region |
| Phenotype as state vs history | Short-term reversible responses differ from long-term biological archives | Response timescale, preservation, dating and calibration |
| Data platform vs source-owned dataset | A hosting service, programme, dataset, edition and describing article have separate identities | Persistent IDs, publisher, edition and source-owner policy |
| Access vs reuse rights | Public reading, API access, downloading and redistribution are not synonymous | Licence text, rights holder and permitted use |
| Ontology similarity vs equivalence | Similar labels do not prove same scientific meaning | Versioned definitions and domain review |

##### Specific scientific distinctions that need strengthening

**Phenotype versus functional trait.** Phenotype is a broad description of observable organism characteristics. A functional trait is a characteristic considered relevant to organism performance or ecological functioning. Functional traits can be phenotypic, but the concepts are not synonymous.

**Plasticity versus adaptation.** Phenotypic plasticity concerns phenotype variation arising from environmental differences for a given genotype or genetic background. Evolutionary adaptation concerns heritable changes shaped by selection across generations. A field observation alone often cannot distinguish these.

**Bioindicator versus biomarker.** A bioindicator may be an organism, assemblage or biological response used to assess environmental conditions. A biomarker is usually a measurable biological characteristic or process associated with exposure, effect or physiological condition.

**Sentinel versus indicator.** Sentinel organisms are selected for monitoring or early warning. Their usefulness depends on the target hazard, exposure pathways, sensitivity and monitoring design.

**Environmental signal versus historical record.** Some phenotypic responses are reversible and short-lived; others may persist developmentally or be preserved in structures such as tree rings. The temporal interpretation needs its own evidence.

**Ecological association versus diagnostic inference.** A species may be associated with a condition without being a sufficiently sensitive or specific diagnostic indicator.

These distinctions are essential to Stage A, but we can preserve them now as provisional conceptual notes.
